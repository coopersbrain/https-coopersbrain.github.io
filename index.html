Meet the Amazon river dolphin

The Amazon river dolphin (Inia geoffrensis) is a freshwater dolphin found throughout parts of the Amazon basin. Its world changes dramatically as rivers rise and fall, so its movements are closely connected to the seasonal flood cycle.

🌊 Freshwater home

They live in the river systems and connected waterways of the Amazon basin rather than the ocean.

🐟 Food follows water

Seasonal changes in water levels change where fish and other prey can be found.

🌳 Flooded forests

When rivers rise, dolphins can move into flooded forest and other areas that become connected to the river.

Where do they migrate?

They do not make a simple, fixed migration from one country to another. Instead, many dolphins shift between the main river channels and seasonally flooded areas as the water rises and falls.

01

High-water season

Rising rivers connect the main channels with floodplain lakes, smaller waterways, and flooded forests. Dolphins can spread into these newly accessible habitats while searching for food.

02

Low-water season

As water levels fall, many dolphins move back toward deeper river channels and other connected waterways. The shrinking floodplain limits where they can travel.

Rain arrives → rivers rise and more habitats become connected.
Floodplain expands → dolphins can explore flooded areas and find prey.
Water falls → accessible habitat shrinks and dolphins concentrate more in river channels.

Why does this matter?

Understanding these seasonal movements is important because a dolphin's habitat is not just one stretch of river. Connected rivers, floodplains, lakes, and forests can all become important at different times of the year. Changes to freshwater systems can therefore affect how animals move and find food.
